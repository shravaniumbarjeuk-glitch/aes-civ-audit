# Auditing Construct Validity in Transformer-Based Automated Essay Scoring

A stylistic-versus-discourse sensitivity audit of transformer scorers on the PERSUADE 2.0 corpus.

**Author:** Shravani Kedarnath Umbarje (Student ID: 5720570)
**Module:** CS907/13 MSc Dissertation, University of Warwick
**Supervisor:** Dr. Ayse Saliha Sunar

## Aim

Automated Essay Scoring (AES) models can match human graders on agreement metrics yet still respond
to surface style rather than argument quality, a validity failure known as **construct-irrelevant
variance (CIV)**. This project builds a diagnostic framework that separates **stylistic CIV** (edits
that preserve meaning) from **discourse-level CIV** (edits that weaken the argument) in two transformer
scorers, and measures how much each type of edit moves the predicted score.

## Headline results

| Model | Test QWK | MAE | Exact acc. | Adjacent acc. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BERT-base-uncased | 0.828 | 0.398 | 62.2% | 98.1% |
| Longformer-base-4096 | 0.856 | 0.336 | 67.5% | 99.0% |

- Both models shift **more** under meaning-preserving stylistic edits than under argument-weakening
  edits (stylistic mean MAS 0.161 / 0.237 versus discourse mean 0.114 / 0.109 for BERT / Longformer).
- The **more accurate model (Longformer) is the less robust one**, driven by syntactic-voice edits.
- Adding length-only filler (the P6 counterfactual) is **associated with** higher scores in both
  models, evidence of length bias.

## Data availability

The **PERSUADE 2.0 corpus** is public and is **not** redistributed here. It is available from its
original release (Crossley et al., 2024) and the Kaggle mirror `nbroad/persaude-corpus-2`. Load it
with `dtype={'essay_id': str}` to avoid identifier corruption.

Two large intermediate files, `perturbations_full_all6.csv` and `perturbations_verified.csv`
(around 200 MB each), exceed GitHub's file-size limit and are **not** committed. They are regenerated
by running `phase3_perturbation_engine.ipynb` and `phase3_sbert_gate.ipynb`, and are available on
Kaggle. Trained model weights (`.pt`) are also large and are referenced rather than committed;
they can be regenerated by the Phase 2 and 2b notebooks or shared on request.

## Reproducing the results

1. Attach the PERSUADE 2.0 dataset on Kaggle and enable a GPU.
2. Run the notebooks in phase order. Each saves its artefacts (weights, predictions, figures).
3. `phase4_robustness_audit.ipynb` reloads the two model weights and produces the audit table and
   figures. All splits use `random_state=42` and both models share identical hyperparameters.
